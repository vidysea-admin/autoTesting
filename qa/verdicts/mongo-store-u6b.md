## Findings

### W1 (Warning) - A holder stalled more than 30 s at one precise point can still leave a refused change in place (reproduced)
`store_mongo_tx.py:100-102` checks `held()`, then `:105,:111` insert the marker and entry, then the data write happens at `store_mongo.py:165` (`_patch`) etc.; nothing re-checks the lease after the write. If the holder A stalls between `held()` and the write for more than the lease (30 s), a successor B takes the lease and recovers (A's journal is deleted), then A's write lands with no journal, A's `transaction()` then raises (`LastAdmin` or `AuthStoreBusy`), and A's `rollback` does nothing because the lease is lost (`:162`). Reproduction (`probe_w.py`, `.work` output in the run): sole admin A, `set_user_groups(A, [])`, successor writer injected before A's database call n (stall): n = 8 leaves zero Admin/CEO members; n = 9 and 10 leave zero members **and `claim_admin` by another account succeeds**; the other 17 call points are clean (A ends `LastAdmin` or `AuthStoreBusy`). It needs a pause of the holder longer than the lease at exactly those calls plus a concurrent writer, and A's operation must be a refused demotion of the sole admin: a compound fault, at most one stray write (each later `_log` fails `held()`). The maker flagged it (manifest item 5). I rule it Warning because the cycle-1 Block was a single fault (a crash) and this is not; but it is the one path to a zero-admin claim that remains, on a CRITICAL criterion. Direction for a later cycle or U9: after each data write re-check `held()` and, if lost, compensate with the same CAS undo; or stamp writes with a fencing epoch. Neither removes the window completely (the compensation can itself be delayed), so the contract owner should record the accepted residual.

### W2 (Warning) - Readers see an unrecovered half-write until the next writer takes the lease (carried A-W3, now measured)
Readers take no lease and do not recover (`store_mongo.py:266-268`). After a crash or store outage in a process that stays up (a Mongo failover, not a process kill), readers see the half-written state until the next writer acquires the lease (a login, a signup, any admin action). Measured with `probe_w.py`: for the refused `update_group` that raised the developer group's permissions, a developer's already-open session resolves a principal with `users.manage` at kill points 17-21 (5 of 31) until a writer runs. A process that restarts is covered: its first `reading()`/`transaction()` runs `_prepare`, which takes the lease and recovers (`store_mongo.py:254`; `AuthStoreBusy` until a dead holder's lease expires, so fail-closed). The window is bounded by the next write, not by 30 s. Suggested cheap closure: `reading()` checks the marker (one `find_one`) and, when present and its owner's lease is expired, recovers first.

### I1 (Info) - Tests cover the recoverer dying only through the rollback path
The sweeps in `test_auth_store_recovery.py` kill the store at every call, which includes the calls of the rollback's `recover(only=owner)`, but no test kills the recoverer at the lease-acquire path (`lease()` `:207`). My nested sweep above covers it (2560 runs, 0 bad), and my own mutants of `recover` die on the existing tests anyway (below), so this is a note, not a gap in the fence.

### I2 (Info) - Availability and leftovers
A crashed holder's lease blocks all writers, and the first `_prepare` of a restarted process, for up to 30 s (`WAIT_SECONDS` 15 < `LEASE_SECONDS` 30, `store_mongo_tx.py:28`); waiters raise `AuthStoreBusy`, fail-closed. Journal entries left when the post-commit sweep fails are inert (`:152-153`, `_docs` filters them). The cold-start multi-client race (cycle-1 W4, `mongo_backend._CACHE`) is U9's and unchanged. M29's wording (`create_exclusive` + `admin_guard`) is realised by a lease plus journal; both cycle-1 checkers ruled it acceptable, the contract owner should record the amendment.
